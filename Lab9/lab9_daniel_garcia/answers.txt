B2 FINAL CONFIG CHOICE + 3-sentence justification (cycles vs. area vs. risk):
Final configuration: C1 (CFG_HAS_FPU = false, ICACHE = 32 lines, DCACHE = 16 lines).
The benchmark program is only 11 instructions at addresses 0x00-0x28, which fits in a single 64-byte cache line, so after one compulsory instruction-cache miss every instruction fetch was a hit (1 miss and 210 hits in our trace) and all five configurations finished in 419 cycles regardless of cache size.
Since C1's flow synthesis area of 4,870,476 um^2 is 16% under the 5,800,000 um^2 limit and all three die heights we placed, from 3,800 to 4,050 um (44.2% to 41.5% utilization), passed detailed placement without errors, C1 carries little risk of a density failure.
Moreover, C1 has a 2 KB instruction cache, which should keep the miss rate low for programs larger than this benchmark.

B9 PREDICTION for Lab 10 (routing): will your chosen point route cleanly?
   State your evidence (frontier spacing, utilization, Lab 8 area).
Yes, we predict that our chosen floorplan will route cleanly.
All three of our floorplans from 44.2% to 41.5% implied utilization passed detailed placement without errors, and we chose the sparsest one at 41.5% to leave the most free space for routing.
Our placement runs also showed nearly the same wirelength at every height (21.4 to 21.7 m HPWL), so the taller die gives more routing room without making the wires longer.
The flow synthesis area is about 10% larger than the lab 8 synthesis area for the same configuration (S increased from 4,036,619 um^2 to 4,465,263 um^2), but C1 still fits under the 5,800,000 um^2 limit with 929,524 um^2 (16%) margin.
However, the resizer inserted about 84,000 buffers during placement, which together with the tap cells raised the post-placement utilization to 50.4% for the H = 4050 um run.
The router also flagged high-fanout nets with about 1,000 pins (GRT-0281), which could cause congestion.
